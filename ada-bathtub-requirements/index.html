ADA bathtub requirements, measured: sections 607, 608 and 610

By Zach Wennstedt, Aging Safely Baths. Checked against the U.S. Access Board's published text of the 2010 ADA Standards on October 15, 2026.

Direct answer: An ADA bathtub needs a clear floor space 30 inches wide along its full length (607.2), a permanent or removable seat whose top sits 17 to 19 inches above the floor (610.2), grab bars on the back and end walls with the upper back bar 33 to 36 inches high (607.4, 609.4), controls on an end wall, a hand shower on a hose of at least 59 inches, and water capped at 120°F (607.6).

Who has to follow these rules

The 2010 ADA Standards for Accessible Design apply to buildings covered by the Americans with Disabilities Act: shops, offices, hotels, medical offices, government buildings and similar places. Section 213.3.6 says that where a bathing facility has bathtubs or showers, at least one bathtub complying with 607 or one shower complying with 608 must be provided. Hotels, care facilities and housing run by public entities have their own scoping rules on how many accessible rooms they need.

A family home is a different situation. The ADA does not reach the private living areas of a house, which is why we wrote a separate page on what actually applies at home. The numbers below are still the best published benchmark for a bathroom that a wheelchair user has to work in, so they make a sensible reference for a home project too.

Clear floor space beside the tub (607.2)

This is the space where a wheelchair parks for a transfer and where a helper stands. It must be at least 30 inches wide and run the full length of the tub. When the tub has a permanent seat at the head end, the space must extend at least 12 inches beyond the head-end wall, so the wheelchair can line up with the seat. A lavatory that meets section 606 may sit at the control end of the clearance.

For comparison, the general wheelchair space in the Standards (305.3) is 30 by 48 inches, and a full turning space (304.3) is a 60-inch circle or a T-shape inside a 60-inch square. Bathrooms that only meet the tub clearance can still be hard to turn around in.

Seats (607.3 and 610)

Every ADA tub needs either a permanent seat at the head end or a removable in-tub seat. Section 610 sets the dimensions.

The 17 to 19 inch range matters because it is close to the seat height of many wheelchairs, which makes a level, sideways slide possible. If your own chair sits higher or lower, the transfer tub guide explains how to match the heights.

Section 609 then sets how every grab bar is built and mounted:

  • Size: round bars 1¼ to 2 inches in outside diameter; other shapes no more than 2 inches across with a perimeter of 4 to 4.8 inches (609.2).
  • Spacing: exactly 1½ inches between the bar and the wall, at least 1½ inches to objects below and at the ends, and at least 12 inches to objects above, except shower controls and fittings, which may be 1½ inches away (609.3).
  • Position: horizontal, 33 to 36 inches above the floor to the top of the gripping surface, apart from the lower back-wall bar at a tub (609.4).
  • Strength: a 250-pound force in any direction at any point on the bar, fasteners, mounting or wall behind it (609.8). Bars must not rotate in their fittings (609.6).

There are two exceptions. Grab bars may be left off a tub in a single-occupant bathing room reached only through a private office, and in residential dwelling units, as long as the walls are reinforced so compliant bars can be added later.

Controls, hand shower and water temperature (607.5 to 607.7, 309.4)

  • Where the controls go: on an end wall, between the tub rim and the grab bar, and between the open side of the tub and the centerline of its width. Drain stoppers are exempt (607.5).
  • How they work: usable with one hand, with no tight grasping, pinching or twisting of the wrist, and no more than 5 pounds of force (309.4).
  • Hand shower: a spray unit on a hose at least 59 inches long that works both as a fixed head and as a hand-held shower, with an on/off control that has a non-positive shut-off. A slide bar must not block the grab bars (607.6).
  • Temperature: 120°F (49°C) maximum at the spray unit (607.6). This protects people with reduced sensation in their legs, who may not feel water that is too hot.
  • Enclosures: doors or screens must not block the controls or the transfer from a wheelchair, and there may be no track on the rim of the tub's open face (607.7). A standard sliding glass tub door on a rim track does not comply.

Section 608: transfer and roll-in showers, side by side

Many people deciding on a tub are also weighing a shower. Here are the three accessible shower types in the Standards, so you can compare footprints.

Thresholds are limited to ½ inch in roll-in showers, and to ½ inch (beveled, rounded or vertical) in transfer showers, with a 2-inch allowance in some existing buildings (608.7). A transfer shower needs a seat (608.4), and its controls go on the side wall opposite the seat, 38 to 48 inches above the shower floor (608.5.1). Seats follow the same 17 to 19 inch height rule as tubs (610.3).

Where walk-in and transfer tubs fit

Read section 607 closely and you will notice it describes a conventional tub: a rim you transfer over, a seat at the head end or on a board, and bars on the walls. It does not mention tubs with doors. That has two practical consequences.

  • In a public or commercial building, do not assume a walk-in tub counts as the required accessible tub. Its seat height, door and grab bars have to be checked against 607 and 610 by the designer, and the local code official has the final word. Section 607.7 is often the sticking point, because nothing may obstruct the transfer from a wheelchair.
  • In a house, the ADA does not apply, so a door-style tub is judged only on whether it works for the bather. A wheelchair transfer tub with an outward-swinging door can be easier to use than a code-compliant standard tub, because the bather slides across at seat height instead of lifting their legs over a rim.

Using these numbers in a house

Five numbers worth copying at home: 30 inches of clear floor beside the tub; a seat top 17 to 19 inches above the floor; grab bars that can take 250 pounds, mounted into blocking, not drywall; a hand shower on a 59-inch hose; and hot water limited to 120°F at the fixture.

The ADA's own exceptions for homes say the same thing in a different way: grab bars may wait, but the reinforcement behind the wall should go in during construction. If you are remodeling, ask for blocking around the whole tub even if you only install one bar today.

Questions people ask us

What seat height does the ADA require for a bathtub?

Section 610.2 of the 2010 ADA Standards puts the top of a bathtub seat 17 inches minimum and 19 inches maximum above the bathroom floor. A removable in-tub seat must be 15 to 16 inches deep, and a permanent seat at the head end must be at least 15 inches deep.

How high do ADA grab bars go beside a bathtub?

The upper bar on the back wall sits 33 to 36 inches above the floor, measured to the top of the gripping surface (609.4). The lower back-wall bar sits 8 to 10 inches above the tub rim (607.4). Each bar must hold a 250-pound force (609.8).

How much clear floor space does an ADA bathtub need?

A space at least 30 inches wide running the full length of the tub (607.2). If the tub has a permanent seat at the head end, that space must run at least 12 more inches past the head-end wall. A lavatory is allowed at the control end of the clearance.

Do the ADA bathtub rules apply to walk-in tubs?

The 2010 Standards describe a standard tub with a seat and grab bars. They do not mention door-style walk-in tubs. In a public building, whether a particular door tub can count as the accessible tub is a call for the designer and the local code official. In a private home the ADA does not apply at all.

What water temperature does the ADA allow at a tub hand shower?

Bathtub shower spray units must deliver water at 120°F (49°C) maximum (607.6). The same section requires a hand shower hose at least 59 inches long that also works as a fixed shower head.
