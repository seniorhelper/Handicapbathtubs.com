Accessible bathing for wheelchair users and people with mobility disabilities

Handicap bathtubs that work from a wheelchair

Transfer tubs, ADA measurements, caregiver technique and funding, explained plainly by the family company that has sold and installed accessible tubs since 2012.

Direct answer: A handicap bathtub lets you bathe without stepping over a high wall. A walk-in tub has a low threshold and a built-in seat for people who can stand briefly. A wheelchair transfer tub has an outward door that opens to seat height so you slide straight across from your chair. In public buildings the ADA sets the rules; in your own home, you choose what fits your body.

Start with how the bather moves today

The right tub depends less on features than on the transfer. Pick the description closest to the person who will bathe.

Slides from a wheelchair

Sits upright and moves sideways to a bed or car seat, alone or with a board. A wheelchair transfer tub is built for exactly that move.

Transfer tubs and techniques

Stands briefly with help

Can stand on at least one leg and pivot with a helper and a gait belt. A transfer tub or a low-threshold walk-in tub can work with one trained helper.

Caregiver-assisted bathing

Needs a lift

Cannot bear weight, or needs two people to move. Plan around a mechanical lift. A roll-in shower is often simpler than a tub.

Roll-in shower guide

Walks, but not over a tub wall

Uses a cane or walker, or has balance problems. A standard walk-in tub with a low step-in and a built-in seat is usually enough.

Questions people ask us

What is a handicap bathtub?

A tub designed so a person with a mobility disability can bathe without stepping over a high wall. The two main types are walk-in tubs with a low threshold and built-in seat, and wheelchair transfer tubs whose outward door opens to seat height so the bather slides across from a chair.

Is a handicap bathtub the same as an ADA bathtub?

Not necessarily. "ADA bathtub" means a tub that meets section 607 of the 2010 ADA Standards, which is required in certain public buildings. Most handicap tubs sold for homes are door-style tubs the ADA text does not describe, and the ADA does not apply inside a private home.

Can I get into a transfer tub from a wheelchair without standing?

Yes, if you can sit upright and slide sideways, alone, with a transfer board or with a helper. You park beside the open door, slide onto the seat, lift your legs in and close the door. If you cannot bear weight or slide, a lift or a roll-in shower is usually safer.

Who pays for a handicap bathtub?

Most people pay privately. Veterans may qualify for VA HISA, SAH or SHA grants. Many state Medicaid waivers fund home accessibility changes, and vocational rehabilitation can help when the change supports a job. Original Medicare generally does not pay.

Do you install, or only ship?

Both. Aging Safely Baths offers full installation in many areas and ships supply-only orders anywhere in the U.S. and to Canada for your own contractor. We confirm what is available at your ZIP or postal code when you call or send the form.

Should I choose a tub or a roll-in shower?

A tub suits people who can sit unsupported and want to soak. A roll-in shower suits people who need a lift, use a shower wheelchair, tire quickly or need the fastest routine. We will tell you honestly if a shower is the better fit.
